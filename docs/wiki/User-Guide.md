# Read and compare constitutions

**Start here:** Countries → country → constitution → version. Public pages show published content. If sign-in is required, use the [[account guide|Account-Guide]].

## Read a text

1. Open **Countries** and select a country.
2. Choose its constitution and the legal version you need.
3. Read the continuous document or follow the table of contents to a provision. The toolbar and visible contents stay accessible as you scroll.
4. Open **Details** to inspect the exact publication, source, verification and related documents.

## Reader controls

**Contents** hides or restores the navigation and remembers your choice. Its own scroll area keeps long lists usable. **Find in text** searches this document, including phrases across inline formatting; use the arrows or Enter/Shift+Enter to move between matches.

**Compare** expands below the toolbar. Select the two publications with **From** and **To**, or swap them. Insertions and deletions show the actual selected texts. Move between changes or choose **Go to changed passage**; **Open full comparison** opens the complete side-by-side view. **Wiki** opens the published introduction and historical context in the same space. Only one of these panels is open at a time.

**Reading preferences** changes text size, configured sentence layout and detail level. **Overview** offers collapsible headings; **Full text** is the initial view. These preferences leave the published wording, formatting, source line breaks and permanent text identities intact.

Hover over or focus a footnote marker for a preview. Select it to open the floating bottom drawer without moving your reading position. Close it with **×**, Escape or an outside click. **Go to footnote** explicitly jumps to the note at the document's end; The stored footnote label remains unchanged. A single reference has a direct return link. For multiple references, one return link opens a choice of the referring passages.

## Search, compare or follow history

| Task | Start here |
| --- | --- |
| Find a passage | **Search**, enter a term and open a result. |
| Compare texts | The reader’s **Compare** panel, or a country’s **Compare** page. |
| Constitutional history | **History**: adoption, commencement, lifecycle events and legal changes in one timeline. |
| Changes in law | **History**, optionally filter to **Amendments**. |

Country and constitution pages offer **Overview** and **History** navigation; the reader includes these links under **Details**. History combines constitution events and amendments in date order. Event labels identify each type; **Show events** optionally filters the same timeline. The centered horizontal line uses small markers for milestones. Hover over a marker or focus it with the keyboard to reveal its label; select it to show the details. On a touch screen, tap a marker. Escape closes the label. Events are grouped by year, with **Jump to year**, previous/next controls and a chronological list. Arrow keys move between milestones; Home/End select the first/last. Approximate dates stay marked and undated records appear separately.

Search filters use jurisdiction, country, version and effective-date controls. Long lists offer an option search. Changing jurisdiction or country clears incompatible filters and explains the adjustment. **Apply filters** updates the address; remove an applied filter chip or choose **Clear filters** to broaden the search. On smaller screens, open **Filters** above the results. A jurisdiction selection searches that jurisdiction's own constitutions; choose descendants separately. Saved unknown or retired version filters remain visible rather than silently broadening the search.

To cite a passage, save the opened provision's address and version ID. Older links stay available. A transcription correction does not itself create a new law.

## Regional and historical archives

Regional and historical version pages offer the same contents/depth controls, source documents, search within the version and neighboring legal versions as national readers. A saved older editorial snapshot keeps its exact text and displays a notice linking to the current text. A unit link opens the containing section so the passage remains visible; the original text is also readable without JavaScript.

The home page links to regions and historical collections. Their breadcrumbs show the recorded jurisdiction context; source-date wording and old status labels remain archival evidence. Typed annotations are distinct from the enacted wording and can be read separately. A constitution's **Amendment register** searches instruments, citations and literal unit labels, with separate instrument/effective date filters. **Curated comparisons** align authored passages across distinct constitutions, identify missing counterparts and preserve topic/text selection in the URL. Saved legacy links keep their exact historical targets.

## Text corrections and legal history

Reading pages emphasize legal versions, effective dates and legal changes. Routine typo and transcription fixes do not add another legal version to the version choices or a legal-change event to the timeline. Editorial details are secondary: staff can open **Structure and editorial details → Text corrections**, or use **Editorial → Text history**, to inspect them. Correction entries remain collapsed until requested.

If you deliberately open a saved older text snapshot, a compact archive notice identifies it and offers **Read current text**. The older link continues to identify the same published text. Article history shows legal versions rather than a feed of typo corrections. On document pages, revision information is available under **Document details and revisions**.

## Check a text's provenance

Open **About** for the site's source labels and verification policy. A version or item marked **Official** has been checked against a cited official source; **Imported** means it came from a corpus and has not been fully checked. Inspect the cited source and version date before relying on a passage. The site is a research aid and does not replace the official publication or legal advice.

## Share or revisit a passage

Use the address of the opened version or unit as a permalink. Save the version ID with a citation, because a country's latest version can change later. The page for an older published snapshot remains distinct from the current one.

## Language and content translation

Use [[Account → Website language|Account-Guide#choose-a-language-or-translate-content]] to change the interface language or request content translation. Authored texts and machine translations are distinct; always check the source for exact wording.

## Site information and privacy

Use **About** for source and verification policy, **Impressum** for the published operator details, and **Datenschutzerklärung** for the published privacy notice. On a first visit, the informational necessary-cookie notice can be dismissed and links to the legal pages. The editorial and account functions require sign-in; published texts are available to public readers when anonymous browsing is allowed.

## If something looks missing

- Check that you selected the intended country, constitution, and version.
- Search covers published content; work waiting for review is not public.
- If a search result does not yet reflect a very recent publication, open the version page directly and try search again after indexing completes.

## Copy text and permanent links

Right-click the constitution text to open the copy menu. It offers the clicked unit and its ancestors, such as sentence, paragraph, article and constitution. Clicking a paragraph does not invent a sentence target. **Copy permalink** stays pinned to the published snapshot being read. **Copy original text** includes stored wording, literal note labels, tables and authored breaks, without interface controls. It copies the published original even when a translation is displayed.

Shift+F10 opens the menu on a focused passage. Arrow keys move between items; Enter or Space selects one, and Escape dismisses it. **Copy** in the toolbar provides the same access on touchscreens and for selected text or the current article. If clipboard access is unavailable, a selectable copy field appears. Separate permalink icons are no longer needed in the text.
